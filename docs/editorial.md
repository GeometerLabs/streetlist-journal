# The Streetlist Journal: editorial guidelines

**Draft for the owner's review** (listed in [review.md](review.md)). For everyone who writes, draws, edits or translates for the
Journal. Short on purpose: read it once before your first piece, and again when you are unsure.

The Journal's line is **"The future, built locally."** It is where art, philosophy, civic ideas and technology meet everyday life in
villages and small towns: places that own and run their own intelligence, with local services, local people and the value staying
where it is made.

## 1. What we publish

Five sections (the source of truth is `content/taxonomy.json`):

| Section | What belongs there |
| --- | --- |
| Technology | Tools, machines and intelligence that a place can own and run itself: how they work, what they need, what they cost |
| Livelihoods | Work and commerce: shops, trades, services, the people who set them up, and the money that stays where it is made |
| Community and civic life | How a village, a town or a ward shares, decides and looks after things together |
| Ideas and philosophy | Questions about technology and everyday life, thought through slowly |
| Art and culture | Pictures, songs, crafts and stories, and the everyday life around them |

Every piece also carries one label that says what kind of piece it is: **Idea** (a proposal nobody has tried yet), **Practical
guide**, **Experiment** (what we ran and what we saw), **Field report** (what happened, with dates), **Research** (careful study with
its sources and limits) or **Opinion** (a view we hold; readers may disagree). Pick the honest one. An idea is never dressed up as a
field report.

## 2. Politics, as we mean it

**The owner's definition (14 Nov 2026): "politics as in the idea of better living standards even in rural areas and better
opportunities".** The Journal's civic side is about better living standards and better opportunities in villages and small towns:
good work close to home, access to services, local ownership, and the chance to build something where you live. We tell it through
ideas, people and examples.

**In scope:** political economy and civic life, written plainly:

- who owns local services, and what changes when a village or a group owns one;
- the commons, cooperatives and producer groups, and how they share costs, surpluses and repairs;
- how a village or a ward decides together: meetings, rules, appeals, records people can question;
- the dignity of local work, and what a new tool does to it;
- what technology does to power and to livelihoods: who gains time, who loses work, who holds the records.

**Out of scope:**

- parties, leaders, candidates, elections and campaigns;
- criticism of a named government, state, department or institution, and of schools or teachers (owner, 4 Nov 2026);
- religion and caste, except as respectful culture (a festival, a craft, a custom, described with care and with the consent of the
  people in it).

**Civic, never partisan.** If a sentence would make sense on a campaign poster, it does not belong here. A useful test: would a
reader from any background in that village feel the piece describes their place fairly? When a public service is part of the
story, describe what it does and how people use it, not who runs it.

## 3. How we write

- **Plain, warm and concrete.** Say what happens, to whom, in which kind of place. The reader's gain comes first, in a plain
  sentence, not a slogan. Our technology appears only as the means, never as the hero.
- **Told positively, never preachy.** We show technology that leaves its value where it is made: owners keep their money, Operators
  keep their margin, communities own their services. We do not lecture or campaign.
- **No claimed results.** Say exactly what each number is and where it comes from. If we have not interviewed anyone, measured
  anything or run anything, the piece says so ("We have not interviewed a salon owner for this piece").
- **Made-up examples are labelled as made up**, in the note under them, and say what they are not.
- **Money** is shown as arithmetic only: what a shop pays, what an Operator keeps (the "margin"). Never a promise of what someone will
  make.
- **Decentralised** means owned and run locally, by people you know. Never tokens, crypto, DAOs or blockchains.
- **Words we never use** (the build checks the site's own words): safe, guaranteed, proves, certified, 100%, bot, earn, income, side
  hustle, passive, limited seats, join now, work from home, founder, founding, leaderboard, streak, revolution, fight, against,
  disrupt. Say "community", never "communal". Two pieces that moved from geometer.dev use "against" in its plain sense ("checked
  against the repository"); they keep every word, and new writing avoids it.
- **People:** Operators and builders are adults (18 or older). Children are never identifiable. Real people appear only with their
  consent, and only as they would want to be seen.
- **Contact** in any piece is hello@geometer.dev only.

## 4. Pictures

- **Every piece has a lead picture** (3:2, at least 1600 px wide), credited to whoever made it ("Photo: Name / Unsplash",
  "Drawing: Name"), with a link to them when they have a page, and with alt text that says what the picture shows in one plain
  sentence. Pages CMS will not save a picture without both.
- **Art direction** is in [image-prompts.md](image-prompts.md): crowded, affectionate, humorous scenes of ordinary people, with
  technology quietly part of daily life. We describe the qualities we want; we never copy or imitate a named artist's work, and no
  prompt names one.
- No readable text, logos or brands in pictures; no person's likeness without consent.
- Until a piece has its picture, its cover shows the piece's companion (a DiceBear face from Streetlist). The companions have no names.

## 5. Languages

- The interface is in English, Hindi, Malayalam and Kannada. A piece is written in one language first.
- A translation is made by a person and **reviewed by a native speaker**, whose name goes in "Reviewed by". Only then is it
  published; until that field is filled the translation stays a draft and off the site. A machine translation is never published.
- Interface words wait for the same review: [strings-review.md](strings-review.md) lists every string beside its English.

## 6. Corrections

If we got something wrong, we say so at the end of the piece, with the date, in the Corrections field. Readers write to
hello@geometer.dev; a person answers.
